# Management Open Innovation opportunities

Each working name denotes an original candidate. All use one model, team-created sample data, a sandbox state store, an allowlisted action and human review for sensitive or unclear changes. The action is inspectable in the prototype. Times below describe scope, not observed implementation performance. [Campus evidence](CAMPUS_PROBLEM_DISCOVERY.md) · [Awarded mechanisms](../references/research/HACKATHON_WINNERS.md) · [Scoring](PROBLEM_SELECTION.md#management-open-innovation)

**Eliminated before scoring.** A general campus FAQ assistant has no operational action. A campus-wide utility control agent depends on sensors, live integrations and authorization. Generic clubs, sports and food-ordering concepts duplicate predefined tracks. A real-data grievance classification system fails the synthetic-data and sensitive-action boundaries.

## Top five detailed concepts

Each candidate shares a local web interface with an incident view, review controls and a visible state ledger. Use a Python API, SQLite transaction boundaries and one team-approved model producing structured tool requests. Provider choice, authentication, quotas and end-to-end latency require validation. An unavailable model routes to a manual structured intake using the same validation and approval path, clearly labeled. These options use sample records and require no real integrations. [FastAPI](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/tutorial/response-model/) · [SQLite](https://sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html) · [Example model function calling](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/function-calling)

### 1. AccessRelay · accessible class continuity

- **Statement and problem.** When a lift or step-free route becomes unavailable, the campus needs a way to identify disrupted teaching sessions and confirm a reachable alternate session. The registrar, facilities, accessibility staff, instructors and affected students have distinct roles. Outage reports and room assignments are maintained independently. [Columbia](https://registrar.columbia.edu/content/protocol-classroom-relocations) · [Gonzaga](https://www.gonzaga.edu/academics/academic-resources/center-for-student-academic-success/disability-access/faculty/elevator-outage-procedure)
- **Loop.** Sense an outage note and known campus state. Think about affected entrances, time and sessions. Choose a location lookup, route lookup or clarification. Act by staging a valid candidate for registrar approval and apply it through a guard at commit. Explain source evidence, dismissed rooms and uncertainty. Verify the new booking, route and instructor acknowledgment. Adapt when access or room state changes. Human reviewers own approvals and unclear cases.
- **90-second demo.** Start at the inaccessible room and show the note. Show a candidate blocked by an unusable entrance and the alternative chosen. Introduce a conflicting reservation just before approval. Show the stale approval refused and the agent returning a new feasible plan. Approve this version, read the new booking back and acknowledge the synthetic instructor notice. Time the recovery and show the audit plus whether hard constraints held.
- **MVP and feasibility.** One small campus fixture, one affected class, three candidate rooms, a route graph, evidence lookup, versioned approval and confirmation. The model supplies interpretation and next action. Use graph reachability plus filters for deterministic eligibility. Test ambiguity, stale proposals and no options. Allocate roughly 4–6 implementation hours for the complete narrow path after authorization, then test. Enhancement is multiple affected sessions. Future scope is institutional integration.
- **PPT and objection.** “The lift is down. Which classes just became unreachable?” Use a before-and-after route diagram and distinguish evidence reasoning from deterministic constraints. Lead metric is valid, acknowledged recoveries. Risk is resembling a scheduler. The state invalidation and recovery trace directly address it. Inspiration includes RiskWise, FROM YOUR EYES and VendorGuard.

### 2. BenchShift · teaching lab disruption recovery

- **Statement and problem.** When a shared teaching instrument goes out of service, lab managers need to recover reservations without assigning incompatible devices or untrained operators. Reservations, equipment capability and deadline notes live in different records. iLab documents reservation tooling, and Bath documents availability and access conditions. [Agilent iLab](https://help.ilab.agilent.com/37179-using-a-core/264636-schedule-equipment) · [Bath](https://www.bath.ac.uk/legal-information/research-facilities-user-agreement-for-staff-and-students/)
- **Loop.** Sense a service note, open reservations and instrument profiles. Think about sample-method descriptions and missing constraints. Act by calling eligibility and availability tools, staging a compatible hold and applying it after lab-manager approval. Explain blocked candidates and deadlines. Verify new reservations and operator coverage. Adapt if the trained staff slot or instrument vanishes, and escalate if equivalence is unknown.
- **90-second demo.** Show three cancelled bookings. One apparently available instrument fails a capability check. A second alternative initially works, then the sample operator becomes unavailable. Reallocate the highest-priority eligible reservation under a documented sample rule. Have the manager approve and show the updated mock ledger and waiting items. Count recovered eligible reservations.
- **MVP and feasibility.** Synthetic profiles and coverage schedule, operator approval page, reservation ledger, rule-based filters and conflict checks. Plan 5–7 build hours, plus testing. Enhancement is multi-lab optimization. Future scope is iLab integration. Use fixed explicit compatibility for the fallback. The major risk is treating inferred method equivalence as fact.
- **PPT and objection.** “Three practicals are waiting on one broken instrument.” Highlight the apparently suitable but invalid backup. The case for originality is recovery across capabilities and operators. Judge may say iLab already books it. Show the disruption and sample eligibility proof. Inspiration includes RiskWise and VendorGuard.

### 3. ExamRelay · accommodation-preserving exam recovery

- **Statement and problem.** A venue closure or sudden proctor absence can invalidate a scheduled exam’s approved conditions. Exam staff, proctors and students need an auditable replacement plan using the same time and seating requirements. Published testing procedures illustrate these dependencies. [University of Alabama](https://ods.ua.edu/faculty-staff/guidance-for-faculty/faculty-guidance-testing-accommodations/) · [Stanford](https://oae.stanford.edu/ctc/faculty-ctc-resources)
- **Loop.** Sense one cancellation and anonymous requirement counts. Think about what changed and select a verification for rooms, staffing or time. Act by staging a filtered plan for an exam-lead approval. Explain exclusions in aggregate. Verify the new seat and shift allocation and adapt if a proctor drops. Escalate missing or sensitive criteria. The model never sets or infers accommodation eligibility.
- **90-second demo.** Start with a cancelled exam room. The nearest alternative lacks a proctor for the extended time block. A lookup finds a second room with a verified steward. After exam-lead approval, show the new seating and staffing ledger and the count meeting the original requirements. Show an aggregate notice.
- **MVP and feasibility.** One exam, two rooms, two staffing shifts and a fixed requirement set, with a transactional swap and no exam content. Plan 6–8 build hours plus testing. Enhancement is simultaneous exams. Future scope is deployment assessment and privacy review. Fallback is human-entered requirements and rule validation. Cross-role policy is the main risk.
- **PPT and objection.** “Moving the exam can change who can sit it.” Overlay the staffing and time constraints. Measure seats whose approved conditions remain valid. Judge may ask why existing testing platforms cannot handle relocation. Show the late disruption and the resulting changed plan. Inspiration includes AegisAgent and VendorGuard.

### 4. DormPivot · hostel service recovery

- **Statement and problem.** During a localized non-emergency water outage, residence staff need to determine which blocks lost service, allocate temporary alternatives within capacity and confirm handoffs with the affected floor representatives. Public residence outage procedures and incident FAQs establish the workflow class. [Howard University](https://thedig.howard.edu/announcements/howard-plaza-towers-water-incident-frequently-asked-questions) · [Maryland](https://drf.umd.edu/services/alerts-utility-outages)
- **Loop.** Sense notices and anonymous occupancy counts. Think through conflicting scope and select a facilities check. Act by staging a capacity-checked plan, approved by the warden. Explain alternatives and affected blocks. Verify coverage and receipt from mock floor reps. Adapt when an alternative reaches quota. Escalate hazards and unknown service status to staff.
- **90-second demo.** Start with a partial outage report and overcrowded alternate facility. Confirm affected units, see the second facility receive an approved plan, then show one pending and one confirmed handoff. Count synthetic groups with feasible coverage.
- **MVP and feasibility.** One incident, two housing blocks and two service alternatives, with a deterministic allocation cap and one confirmation view. Plan 4–6 build hours plus tests. Enhancement is load-balancing or other non-emergency service categories. Future scope is verified workflow integration. Fallback is manual scope entry and the same capacity checks.
- **PPT and objection.** “The notice went out. Which floors still have no usable option?” Show a floor-level coverage diagram and metric. Judge may see a notification system. Highlight the validated allocation and tracked receipt. Inspiration includes AegisAgent and Eco-Metropolis.

### 5. ReUse Dispatch · verified campus surplus handoff

- **Statement and problem.** When university assets become surplus, stores teams need to match eligible departmental demand and verify a transfer despite incomplete condition notes or a failed pickup. Existing surplus programs already address inventory and disposition. [Stanford](https://ora.stanford.edu/ora-units/property-management-office/surplus-property-overview)
- **Loop.** Sense inventory, requests and condition notes. Think about suitability and select an inspection or policy lookup. Act by staging an approved claim. Explain which evidence supports the recipient. Verify acceptance and inventory movement. Adapt on rejection or expired pickup. Stores controls release, recipients confirm receipt, and ambiguous condition goes to a reviewer.
- **90-second demo.** List a retired desk with inconsistent condition notes. The agent requests a condition check and offers it to an eligible department. The department declines, the agent replans and an authorized staff member confirms the next route. Verify the new inventory owner and count one complete transfer.
- **MVP and feasibility.** One asset class, condition records, recipient criteria, atomic claims and acknowledgment. Plan 5–7 build hours plus tests. Enhancement is batch transfer proposals. Future scope is procurement integration and validated avoided-spend estimates. Fallback is manual inspection plus the same claim ledger.
- **PPT and objection.** “The item was listed twice. It still never reached the department that needed it.” Show the failed and completed transfer. Count verified receipts. Judge may see a surplus marketplace. Show traceable condition and reallocation with the audit. Inspiration includes KindThreads and Spoon Share.
