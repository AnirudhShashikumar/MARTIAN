# MARTIAN 1.0 operating instructions

- Start each session with [tracking/STATUS.md](tracking/STATUS.md). Recompute the usable time in IST from the latest organizer deadline and scheduled breaks. Update status, decisions, tasks and risks after meaningful changes.
- Confirmed track is **Management Track 4 · Open Innovation**, under the statement packet's **Agentic AI for Campus Wellness** theme. The specific statement needs user acceptance and organizer approval. Use team-created sample data, keep decisions auditable, protect privacy, explain policy and fairness checks, and escalate unclear or sensitive actions to a human. Keep real college systems, real payments and personal data out of the prototype. Declare all third-party tools and preserve original event work.
- Aim for a top-10 Round 1 shortlist first. Rank sources in descending order from latest organizer instructions to official statements, rubric, PPT template and submission instructions, rulebook, schedule, team preferences and internal recommendations. Record conflicts.
- Until the official statements arrive, limit work to general readiness. Preserve source files as received. Keep project-specific code and the final deck out of Phase 0.
- On receipt, enter Round 1 immediately. Preserve statement wording in [context/PROBLEM_STATEMENTS.md](context/PROBLEM_STATEMENTS.md). Extract requirements and rank every eligible option using the official rubric where applicable, or the labeled internal framework. Show the strongest option, the top three if available, and key trade-offs. Obtain the user's approval before committing to a statement or a major strategy change. Organizer approval is required to change an assigned or approved statement.
- After selection, verify the campus workflow and competitive gap, compare three concepts, and design one feasible core journey. Ground recommendations in qualification impact, differentiation, buildability and technical defensibility. Label competitor predictions as hypotheses and record research URLs, publication dates and findings.
- Show exactly where the agent observes, understands, plans, acts, verifies and adapts. Specify tool permissions, state, failure recovery, human approvals, privacy and auditability. Prefer one agent unless specialization provides a measurable advantage.
- Build the Round 1 narrative around the official rubric and presentation rules. Prepare slide copy and notes, architecture and workflow diagrams, a timeboxed pitch and 15 tough judge questions. Run the five-perspective review before finalizing. Label planned, implemented and validated claims.
- Treat the rubric’s working Round 1 PoC and the event flow’s post-shortlist prototype slot as a timing conflict that needs organizer resolution. Begin work after the user approves the statement, organizers approve it and confirm the applicable build window. Build and test the core flow first, document contributions and third-party licenses, and reserve time for integration and an honestly labeled live demo and fallback.
